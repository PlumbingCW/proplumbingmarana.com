Sewer Line Leak Detection in Marana, AZ

A sewer line leak in Marana, AZ usually shows up as gurgling drains, a sewer smell, slow drains in more than one fixture, or a wet or sunken spot in the yard. A camera inspection and a pipe locator can confirm where the break is, often without digging first.

The first moves are simple. Stop running water, note which fixtures are affected, and find out whether the problem is in your private lateral or in the public main. This guide explains the signs, the detection methods and who owns what.

What to do in the first ten minutes

Stop using water in the house if drains are backing up. That means no showers, no laundry and no dishwasher until you know what is happening. Every gallon you send down a blocked line has to go somewhere.

If sewage is coming up into a tub, shower or floor drain, call our 24/7 line. Keep children and pets out of the area, and do not touch the water with bare hands.

Signs the sewer line is leaking or blocked

One slow drain is usually local. A pattern across the house points to the main line.

Gurgling from drains

Air pushed back by a blockage makes bubbling sounds in a toilet or tub when another fixture drains.

Sewer odor indoors or outside

A steady rotten smell near a cleanout, a floor drain or part of the yard suggests a leaking joint or break.

Several drains slow at once

If the toilet, shower and washer all drain poorly, the problem is likely downstream of all of them.

Drain backs up into the tub or washer

Water from the washer appearing in the tub is a classic sign of a blocked main line.

Wet, sunken or unusually green ground

Leaking sewage can soften soil and feed grass. A dip in the ground may mean the soil has washed into the pipe.

Roots or repeated clogs

A line that clogs every few weeks often has roots, a belly or a cracked section.

Who handles sewer service around Marana

Marana Water Reclamation serves Saguaro Bloom and North Marana. Continental Reserve, Hartman Vistas and the Cortaro area are served by Pima County Regional Wastewater Reclamation, which you can reach at (520) 724-6609. If you are in one of those neighborhoods, that is the number to call when the public main appears to be the problem.

The Town states that it is responsible for sewer mains in Town rights of way and easements. The homeowner maintains the private lateral from the house to the public main, so a clog or break on that stretch is your repair. A camera can show which side of the property line the trouble sits on.

In Picture Rocks, confirm whether your property is on public sewer or a septic system before you plan anything. Our Picture Rocks plumber page explains how service works there, and Pima County's onsite wastewater program regulates septic systems.

How a plumber finds the problem

Good detection starts with the least invasive method and narrows from there.

  1. 1

    Interview and quick tests

    The plumber asks which fixtures are affected and when, then runs water at several points to see how the line responds.

  2. 2

    Camera inspection

    A small waterproof camera on a flexible cable goes in through a cleanout. It shows cracks, roots, offsets, sags and debris on a monitor.

  3. 3

    Locating the problem

    A transmitter in the camera head sends a signal that a handheld receiver picks up above ground. The plumber marks the spot and the depth.

  4. 4

    Flow or pressure style tests

    For suspected leaks, the line may be filled with water or tested to see whether the level drops. The exact method depends on access.

  5. 5

    A written finding

    You should get a plain description of the problem, its location and the repair options, along with a price before work starts.

Camera and locating are not the same thing

A camera shows what the inside of the pipe looks like. A locator shows where that camera is underground. You usually need both before anyone digs. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what you should see in the report.

If the inspection finds a break, a belly or heavy root damage, the next step is a repair plan. Our sewer line repair page covers the options, from spot repairs to replacement.

What to have ready before the visit

A few notes help the plumber find the problem faster.

  • A list of which drains are slow, gurgling or backing up.
  • The location of the cleanout, if you know where it is.
  • When the problem started and whether it has happened before.
  • Any recent landscaping, tree work or digging near the line.
  • Whether the house is on public sewer or a septic system.
  • Photos of any wet spots, sinkholes or odors in the yard.

Monsoon runoff and your sewer line

Heavy summer rain can saturate soil and make a leaking sewer line harder to distinguish from a surface runoff problem. Sewage smell in a wet spot is a strong clue, but a camera is the sure test.

Never direct storm runoff into a sewer cleanout, and keep the cleanout cap secure. Water that enters the sewer from outside adds load to the line and can cause backups. If a heavy storm is followed by a backup, mention it when you call. Any repair that follows detection is plumbing work under the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) the Town of Marana has adopted, so keep the camera recording to show the inspector and to compare quotes.

Septic caveat

Not every property in the Marana area is on a public sewer. Some are on septic systems. If yours is, a camera inspection of the house line is still useful, but the tank and drain field need a septic specialist. Confirm your setup before you call anyone.
