Low Water Pressure in Marana, AZ: What to Check First

Low water pressure in a Marana, AZ home usually comes from one of four places: a partly closed valve, scale in an aerator or showerhead, a failing pressure reducing valve, or a leak on the line feeding the house. Screw a pressure gauge onto an outdoor hose bib and read it. Most homes sit between about 40 and 80 psi.

If only one faucet is weak, the fixture is the suspect. If the whole house is weak, look at the valves, the regulator and the supply line, and call Marana Water to ask whether anything is happening in your area.

Measure it before you guess

A pressure gauge with a hose-thread fitting is inexpensive and takes five minutes. Weak feeling is not the same as low psi.

  1. 1

    Turn everything off

    Close all taps, stop the washer and dishwasher, and make sure no irrigation zone is running.

  2. 2

    Attach the gauge

    Screw it onto an outdoor hose bib, ideally the one closest to where the water enters the house, and open the bib fully.

  3. 3

    Read the static pressure

    Most homes fall between about 40 and 80 psi. Below 40 feels weak, and above 80 is hard on fixtures and appliances.

  4. 4

    Read it again with water running

    Open an indoor tap and watch the needle. A big drop means the supply or the regulator cannot keep up.

  5. 5

    Write both numbers down

    Any plumber or water provider will ask for them. A second reading at a different bib shows if the problem is local.

Desert scale and the Marana water systems

Town of Marana Water runs several systems, and their published hardness ranges from roughly 3 to 14 grains per gallon depending on the neighborhood. The Town says hardness poses no health risk but leaves deposits on fixtures. In a 12 GPG area, expect aerators to clog sooner than in a 5 GPG area, so clean them first.

The Town says several different water providers operate within its limits. Check your bill to see who supplies you before you call anyone. For leaks or sewer issues after hours, weekends and holidays, Marana Water lists (520) 235-4381.

Pressure questions about the street supply go to Marana Water. If a hose bib at the meter reads normal and your indoor taps are weak, the cause is likely inside your property line.

Causes in order of how often they turn up

Start at the top. The cheap checks come first.

Partly closed valve

The main shutoff, the meter valve or an angle stop under a sink may not be fully open. A valve left half-turned after a repair is a classic cause.

Scale in aerators and showerheads

Hard water crusts the small holes. Soak the parts in vinegar and brush them. If pressure returns, the fixture was the problem.

Failing pressure reducing valve

The bell-shaped regulator near the main steps down street pressure. As it wears, pressure can swing high or sag low.

Clogged inlet strainers

Small screens in faucets, washer hoses and the regulator trap grit. Turn water off before removing them.

Leak on the service line

Water escaping between the meter and the house robs pressure from the whole home. It often shows up as a spinning meter with everything off.

Old galvanized pipe

Older steel pipe narrows inside from rust. It is less common in newer desert homes but possible in older ones.

Is it your pipes or your water provider?

Pressure that is low at the meter and low at every neighbor's house is a provider matter. Marana Water can tell you whether a main repair, flushing or system work is under way. You can reach them at (520) 382-2570 from 8 AM to 4 PM.

If your gauge reads fine at the meter side but poor inside, the cause is on your property. A reading taken before and after the regulator, when a plumber can reach both points, separates the two cleanly.

Two safety notes

Do not crank an adjustable regulator up to make showers stronger. Pressure over about 80 psi can wear out fixtures and hoses and cause leaks. And if the ground near your meter is soggy with no rain or irrigation, shut the main off and call, since a buried leak can undermine soil.

Weak hot water only

When cold is strong and hot is weak, the problem is between the tank and the tap. A partly closed heater shutoff, scale in the heater outlet or a clogged mixing valve can all choke the flow. If the heater is in a hot attic or garage, take care working near it, and have a plumber handle anything that involves draining the tank or power.

Our water heater repair team can look at hot-side flow when the cold side is fine.

Do these in order before calling

A short checklist saves a visit.

  • Open the main shutoff and meter valve fully.
  • Compare hot and cold at the same faucet.
  • Clean the aerator on the weakest tap.
  • Take a gauge reading with all water off.
  • Do the meter test: all water off, look for movement.
  • Ask a neighbor whether their pressure changed too.

When to call a plumber

Call when the gauge reads under 40 or over 80, when the regulator is humming or leaking, or when the meter moves with everything off. A regulator swap and a buried line leak both need proper tools and sometimes a permit.

A suspected buried leak calls for water service line repair or leak detection to find the spot before digging. Call 811 at least two working days before any digging of your own. Homes in Picture Rocks should confirm their water provider first, because not every property uses the Town system.

The 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) that the Town of Marana adopted expects pressure to stay within a safe range at the fixtures, which is why a pressure reducing valve is worth testing before you blame the meter.
