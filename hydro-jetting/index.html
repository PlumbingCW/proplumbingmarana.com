Snaked a few weeks ago and it is clogging again

This is the most common reason people call us for jetting. A cable machine drills a channel through the blockage, the water drains, and the line looks fixed. But the grease, scale and sludge on the pipe wall stay in place and start collecting new debris right away.

A second pattern is a snake that got stuck, would not advance, or came back with roots wrapped on the head. That tells you the obstruction is firm or the pipe has an offset or break, and pushing harder is the wrong response.

If your drain keeps clogging every few weeks, jetting removes the buildup that the snake left behind. If roots or damage are the cause, the water clears the root mass, but the entry point still has to be found and repaired.

How a jetting visit goes

Each step has a reason. Skipping the first one is how a bad pipe gets damaged.

  1. 1

    Find the access

    We locate a cleanout or pull a fixture so the hose has a clean way into the line. Slab homes often have a single cleanout near the foundation.

  2. 2

    Look before pressure

    When the history suggests damage, such as a stuck snake or an old line, a camera runs first and shows what the water will face.

  3. 3

    Cut the blockage

    A nozzle with forward and rear jets pulls itself down the line. Forward jets break the plug and the rear jets scour the walls on the way back.

  4. 4

    Flush and confirm

    We run the line until the water clears and then test the fixtures. A follow-up camera pass can verify the pipe is clean and show its condition.

Where jetting is the right call and where it is not

Pressure is only an advantage in pipe that can take it. Know which column your line belongs in.

Jetting fits well

  • Grease buildup in kitchen and main lines
  • Soft mineral scale and sludge coating pipe walls
  • Root masses in an intact pipe, followed by a repair plan
  • Repeat clogs that cable snaking never fixes
  • Lines that need a thorough clean before lining or inspection

Camera or other method first

  • Cracked, collapsed or separated pipe
  • Very old or brittle pipe, including some cast iron
  • A snake that stuck or hit a hard stop and will not advance
  • Lines where the material and condition are unknown
  • Heavy hard scale that may need mechanical cutting

Roots, grease and Marana water

Roots are a general problem for buried lines wherever trees and shrubs grow near them. They find joints, grow inside, and hold paper and grease. Jetting clears the mass, but roots grow back, so a line with roots may need repeat service or a repair to stop them.

Hard water adds to the story. Local hardness runs roughly 3 to 14 grains per gallon depending on the neighborhood, and scale roughens pipe walls so grease clings. Check your provider on your water bill since several water systems serve the Town.

Most newer Marana homes sit on slabs, so the main line leaves the house under the concrete. Reaching it is a job for a cleanout and a hose, and that is a good fit for jetting when the pipe is sound.

Camera first when the pipe is in question

A camera adds one step and removes a guess. If we see an offset joint, a crack or heavy root intrusion, we may recommend repair before or instead of jetting. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what the picture shows.

If the camera shows a failing pipe, the next move is repair, and sewer line repair lays out the options. Jetting a line that cannot hold up simply moves the failure to a worse moment.

What to do before you call about a jetting job

  • Note which fixtures back up and when, so the plumber can tell a branch clog from a main line problem.
  • Find your cleanout cap and clear the area around it, but do not open it if water is rising.
  • Stop running water, laundry and dishwashers until the line is cleared.
  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaner in first, because it can burn skin during the job.

Commercial kitchens in Marana produce grease at a scale homes never see, and their lines clog faster. Jetting is a regular maintenance tool for them. Beyond the Town limits, we also serve Picture Rocks, where permits for repair work go through Pima County rather than the Town.
