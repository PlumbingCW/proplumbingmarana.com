Water Softener and Filtration in Marana, AZ

A water softener in Marana, AZ swaps the calcium and magnesium that make water hard for sodium or potassium, so scale stops building on fixtures, dishes and water heaters. We plan, install and service softeners and filtration, and we are licensed and insured.

The right size depends on where you live. Marana Water runs several separate systems, and their hardness differs more than most people expect.

Reading ppm and GPG without getting it wrong

Water reports use two units. Parts per million (ppm) is the Town's headline figure, and grains per gallon (GPG) is what most softeners use. One grain per gallon is about 17.1 ppm, so 215 ppm works out to roughly 12 GPG.

The Town's own FAQ tells customers to check which unit a softener uses. Mixing them up can leave a unit sized for a fraction of the real load, or far more than it needs.

What the Town says about hardness

Arizona water passes through soils rich in calcium and magnesium, and the Town states that hardness poses no health risk. The effect is deposits on bath fixtures, dishes and glassware, plus scale in water heaters. We do not diagnose health effects of water. That is outside what a plumber can answer.

Salt-based softener or salt-free conditioner

They are not the same thing, and they do different jobs.

Salt-based softener

  • Removes hardness minerals by ion exchange
  • Prevents scale on fixtures and in heaters
  • Needs salt refills and a drain for regeneration
  • Adds a small amount of sodium or potassium to the water

Salt-free conditioner

  • Changes how minerals form crystals, rather than removing them
  • No salt and no regeneration drain
  • May reduce scale buildup but does not give soft-water feel
  • Results vary, so set expectations honestly

What a softener does and what it does not

A softener lowers hardness. It does not purify water, remove bacteria, or turn tap water into drinking water. Filtration is a separate stage with its own purposes, such as taste, sediment or chlorine reduction.

Filtration needs a water test before anyone picks a system. We do not name products or brands, and we will say plainly when a filter will not do what someone hopes it does.

Sizing a softener from hardness and household

Sizing is arithmetic, not guesswork.

  1. 1

    Find your hardness in GPG

    Use the Town table for your system. Convert ppm to GPG if needed.

  2. 2

    Count daily water use

    A common planning figure is gallons per person per day, multiplied by the number of people at home.

  3. 3

    Multiply to get grains per day

    Hardness times gallons used gives the grains the softener removes each day.

  4. 4

    Pick a capacity and regeneration cycle

    A unit sized so it regenerates every few days wastes less salt and water than one that regenerates daily.

What a planned softener install needs

A few things have to be in place before a softener works.

  • A cold water supply line where the softener can tie in, usually before the water heater
  • A drain connection for the regeneration cycle, with an air gap
  • A nearby electrical outlet for the control valve
  • Floor space for the tank and a salt brine tank, with a bypass valve for service
  • Outdoor hose bibs and irrigation left on hard water if you prefer, which saves salt

Service on the softener you already have

Softeners wear in familiar ways. Salt bridges block the brine tank, resin beads break down, control valves stall, and drain lines clog. Hard water that returns after a long soft stretch is a common sign, as is a salt level that never drops.

We service existing units, including water conditioner repair. Good records help, so note the model and the settings before the call.

What to do before you call about a softener

  • Find your water system name on the Town's hardness table or your water bill.
  • Note how many people live in the home, since that affects softener sizing.
  • Check where a drain and a power outlet sit near the water line entry.
  • Decide whether you need softening, filtration for taste, or both before you compare quotes.

Softened water and the rest of the plumbing

Scale is what shortens the life of a tank. A softener cuts it, which helps a tankless water heater most of all, since its heat exchanger is the first place scale causes trouble. A softener install also touches the plumbing around it, which our plumbing installation work covers.

A softener ties into your supply and needs a drain connection built to the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) the Town of Marana has adopted, so ask the installer how the brine drain and bypass will be set up.
