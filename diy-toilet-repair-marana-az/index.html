DIY Toilet Repair in Marana, AZ

Most toilet repair in Marana, AZ is a small job you can do yourself: a flapper that leaks, a chain that is too tight or loose, a fill valve that will not shut off, or a clog that a plunger clears. Shut the water at the wall valve first, then fix the one part that is failing.

A wobbling base, water around the bottom or a toilet that backs up other drains is a different story, and those cases are better handled by a plumber.

Start with a dye test for a running toilet

A toilet that refills by itself is wasting water into the bowl. A quick test shows if the flapper is the leak.

  1. 1

    Lift the tank lid

    Set it somewhere safe on a towel, since porcelain chips and cracks easily.

  2. 2

    Add dye

    Drop in a few drops of food coloring or a dye tablet. Do not flush.

  3. 3

    Wait fifteen to twenty minutes

    Keep everyone from flushing this toilet.

  4. 4

    Look at the bowl

    Color in the bowl means water is leaking past the flapper. No color means the flapper is sealing, so look at the fill valve and overflow tube.

  5. 5

    Close the wall valve and flush

    This drains the tank so you can reach the flapper.

Who maintains what when a toilet backs up

The Town of Marana says it is responsible for sewer mains in Town rights of way and easements, while the homeowner maintains the private sewer lateral from the house to the public main. If a toilet backs up and other drains do too, the blockage may be on your side of that connection. Call a plumber before you call the Town.

Sewer service varies by area. Marana Water Reclamation serves Saguaro Bloom and North Marana, and Pima County Regional Wastewater Reclamation serves Continental Reserve, Hartman Vistas and Cortaro. In Picture Rocks, confirm whether your property is on public sewer or a septic system, since that changes who handles a main-line backup.

The Town also lists hardness about 14 GPG on average on the airport system and about 12 GPG on several others. Expect scale on flush parts sooner than in softer areas.

Tank parts you can swap

Parts are cheap and found at any hardware store. Bring the old ones to match sizes.

Flapper

A rubber flapper hardens and curls with age. Unhook the ears from the overflow tube posts and the chain from the handle arm, then clip on a matching one.

Chain

Leave about half an inch of slack. Too tight keeps the flapper open, and too loose may not lift it.

Fill valve

If water keeps rising into the overflow tube, adjust the float. If that fails, replace the valve. Shut the water, drain the tank, unthread the supply, and fit the new valve.

Water level

The tank should fill to the mark on the tank or about an inch below the top of the overflow tube.

How hard water changes toilet repair

Mineral scale builds where water sits, which includes the flapper seat, the fill valve and the small jets under the bowl rim. A rough seat lets water seep past even a new flapper. Wipe the seat with a nonmetal scrubber and vinegar before you fit a new part.

White crust at the valve or supply line connection is also the first thing to look for if a part seems stuck. Do not use force on a seized fitting, since a cracked tank is a bigger repair than the one you started with.

Plunging a clog the right way

Use a flange plunger made for toilets, not the flat cup type used on sinks.

  1. 1

    Cover the drain opening

    Fit the plunger so the flange sits inside the drain hole and the cup seals around it.

  2. 2

    Push gently first

    The first push can send water splashing. Then pump with steady strokes for ten to twenty seconds.

  3. 3

    Pull sharply on the last stroke

    A final pull often breaks the clog loose.

  4. 4

    Test with a small amount of water

    Pour in a bucket of water before flushing. If the bowl drains, flush once to confirm.

  5. 5

    Skip chemical drain cleaners

    They can burn skin, damage fixtures and endanger anyone who has to work on the drain afterward. A toilet auger is safer if the plunger fails.

The wobbly toilet and the wax ring

A toilet that rocks may only need its floor bolts tightened a little at a time, since overtightening cracks the base. If it still moves, or water shows around the base, the wax ring under it has failed. Replacing it means shutting the water, draining the tank and bowl, lifting the toilet off the flange and setting a new ring. It is a heavy two-person job and the flange must be in good shape.

Before you start any toilet repair

These habits prevent most mishaps.

  • Close the angle stop on the wall by turning clockwise. If it will not turn or drips, stop and call.
  • Flush to empty the tank, then sponge out the rest.
  • Put down towels and keep a bucket handy.
  • Take a photo of the parts before you take them apart.
  • Never over-tighten plastic nuts or porcelain bolts.
  • If you smell sewage gas or the floor feels soft, stop.

When to call a plumber

Call when a toilet keeps clogging every few weeks, when water leaks at the wall valve or the base, or when the toilet and a tub or sink back up together. A repeat clog may point to a line problem that a plunger will not solve, which is where drain cleaning with the right equipment comes in.

Our toilet repair visits cover parts replacement and resets, and homes in Picture Rocks are within our service area too. A toilet set and sealed correctly follows the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) the Town of Marana has adopted, so use a new wax ring and the right closet bolts rather than reusing worn parts.
