A pipe has burst and water is still running

Take the first minutes calmly and in order. Our 24/7 line is open, and a quick call while you work through this list helps us plan the repair.

  1. Close the main shutoff valve at the house or at the meter. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Open the lowest faucet in the house and the highest to drain what is left in the lines.
  3. Switch off the water heater. Cut power to wet rooms at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  4. Stay away from outlets, appliances, and anything electrical standing in water.
  5. Move valuables and soak up standing water if it is safe to do so.
  6. Photograph and film the damage before cleanup.

Document it now for your insurer

Sudden pipe bursts are often treated differently from slow leaks, so a clear record helps. Do this before you move too much.

  • Wide photos of each wet room, then close-ups of the broken pipe and any stains.
  • A short video of the water source before and after it is shut off.
  • The time you first noticed it and the time you closed the main.
  • A photo of the meter dial after shutoff.
  • A list of damaged belongings with rough age and purchase detail.
  • Keep receipts for any emergency supplies and for our work.

Why pipes crack in a desert town

Tucson sees about 12 days a year at or below 32 degrees, so a freeze is brief and rare but real. Water expands as it freezes, and that stress is enough to split a pipe wall. Exposed outdoor lines, hose bibs, irrigation backflow assemblies, and anything in an unheated garage are the usual victims, and the split often shows only after the thaw.

Heat does damage too. In summer, attics get very hot, and supply lines up there expand and contract with each use. Fittings can loosen or crack over the years. If a pipe in your attic fails, water usually shows up first as a ceiling stain.

Pressure and water chemistry matter as well. Excess pressure strains joints and valves, and Marana's hard water, roughly 3 to 14 grains per gallon depending on the neighborhood, leaves scale that can restrict flow and corrode some fittings.

A cracked or broken pipe versus a slow pipe leak

A cracked or broken pipe throws water quickly, and the signs are loud: spraying, a rush of sound in the wall, or a sudden loss of pressure. A pipe leak is slower, such as a pinhole weeping at a fitting or a seep that stains drywall over weeks.

Both need repair, but the first has to be stopped at once. If the meter moves and you cannot see a source, read about leak detection to find where the water is going.

After the water is off: what happens next

Once the flow is stopped, we locate the break, cut back to solid pipe, and make the repair. Then we restore pressure slowly, test every joint we touched, and check nearby fittings for the same weakness.

Water damage is the next step. Drywall, insulation, and flooring that stayed wet can grow mold, so drying matters. We handle the plumbing, and we can tell you what we see so your insurer or a restoration company knows what to address.

Permits depend on the job

A small repair may not need a permit, while replacing sections of pipe can. Ask Town of Marana Development Services at (520) 382-2600, or Pima County for unincorporated areas such as Picture Rocks.

When a burst is a sign of something bigger

One failed pipe can be bad luck. Two or three on the same system within a year often means the pipe is wearing out, and a water service line repair or a full replacement may be the better value.

We look at the age, material, and condition of what we see and tell you plainly if more failures are likely. A permanent burst pipe repair in Marana is expected to meet the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) the Town has adopted, so ask whether the fix is temporary or code-compliant and whether a permit applies.
