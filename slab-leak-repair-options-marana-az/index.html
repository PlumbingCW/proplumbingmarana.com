Slab Leak Repair Options for Marana, AZ Homes

A slab leak in Marana, AZ is fixed one of four ways: open the slab and repair the spot, reroute the line through the attic or walls, repipe the house, or coat the inside of the pipe with epoxy. The right choice depends on how many leaks there are, what the pipe is made of, and how much floor you are willing to open.

Before choosing, confirm the leak is real and under the concrete. Many suspected slab leaks turn out to be a leaking fixture, a stuck irrigation valve or a water heater connection, and those cost far less to fix.

Prove it is a slab leak first

Run through these checks before anyone saws concrete. They take less than an hour and can save a lot of money.

  • Turn off every fixture, appliance and irrigation zone, then look at the water meter. A moving dial or spinning leak indicator with everything off means water is escaping somewhere.
  • Close the main shutoff to the house and watch the meter again. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and your shutoff, which is the service line and not a slab leak.
  • Feel the floor for a warm spot, especially on tile. A hot-water line leaking under the slab can warm the floor above it.
  • Listen at night for a faint hiss or running sound with all water off.
  • Look for damp baseboards, a musty smell, lifted flooring, or water at the edge of the garage slab.
  • Check the water heater, toilets and hose bibs for slow drips that could explain the meter.

How a plumber pinpoints the spot

Pinpointing matters because it decides how big the hole is. Technicians use pressure tests to find which line is losing water, then electronic listening equipment, thermal imaging on hot lines, and sometimes tracer gas to mark the exact point. Our leak detection work follows that order so the cut, if one is needed, is small.

Ask to see the readings or a marked floor before any cutting starts. A confident answer includes which line is leaking, hot or cold, and roughly how far from a wall.

Permits and codes for slab work in Marana

Marana's Town Code section 7-1-2 adopts the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) with local amendments. Rerouting or replacing supply piping falls under that code, so ask your plumber how the work will meet it.

Permit rules vary by job, so call Town Development Services at (520) 382-2600, open for customer service Monday through Friday 8 AM to 5 PM, before work begins. Inspections run 7 AM to 3 PM, which matters if you are scheduling around a day off.

In unincorporated Pima County, such as much of Picture Rocks, the county's Do I Need a Permit page lists new pipe work as requiring a permit. Confirm with the county rather than assume the Town's rules apply.

Spot repair or reroute: the usual first decision

Most Marana slab leaks come down to these two. Rerouting avoids the slab, while spot repair keeps your existing layout.

Spot repair under the slab

  • Cut the concrete over the leak, replace the bad section, patch the slab
  • Needs floor covering removed above the cut
  • Works well for a single clean failure
  • Leaves old pipe under the slab that may leak again elsewhere

Reroute through attic or walls

  • Run new pipe overhead and abandon the old under-slab section
  • Avoids breaking concrete
  • Makes future repairs easier to reach
  • Attic pipes see summer heat, so insulation and routing matter

What an epoxy liner can and cannot do

Epoxy lining cleans the inside of a pipe and coats it with a resin so pinholes seal. It needs a continuous run with workable access at both ends. It is a poor choice when a pipe has split, a joint has failed, or the line has many sharp turns. Ask the installer to show a camera view of the pipe condition first.

What to do while you wait for the repair

A slab leak can run for weeks without showing. These steps limit the damage.

  1. 1

    Close the main water valve

    If the floor is wet or flooring is lifting, shut the house main. Water running under a slab can undermine soil and damage flooring.

  2. 2

    Move belongings off the floor

    Pull rugs, boxes and furniture legs off wet areas. Photograph everything before and after.

  3. 3

    Document the meter

    Photograph the meter dial with everything off, then again an hour later. Your insurer and your plumber will both ask for it.

  4. 4

    Call our 24/7 line

    Call (855) 575-2890 and describe the warm spot, hiss or wet edge. Keep the main off if water is visible.

Insurance: a hedge, not a promise

Homeowners policies often cover sudden water damage but may exclude the cost of repairing the pipe itself, or treat a slow leak as wear. Some policies cover access costs such as opening the slab and others do not.

Read your policy for terms like slab, foundation and tear-out, and call your insurer before work begins if you plan to file. Ask the plumber for a written description of the cause and the repair, since adjusters usually want it.

Questions worth asking any plumber

These help you compare quotes fairly.

  • How did you confirm the leak is under the slab, and which line is it?
  • Which options fit my pipe, and why did you rule others out?
  • How much flooring will be opened, and who restores it?
  • Is the contractor's license class right for the work? Check it at roc.az.gov.
  • Who pulls the permit and schedules the inspection?
  • Is the price clear before work starts, and does it list the access and patch work?

When to call a plumber

Call for any confirmed or suspected slab leak. Cutting concrete, rerouting supply lines and judging pipe condition are not weekend DIY jobs, and a wrong guess about the location can mean extra holes in your floor.

For homes in Picture Rocks, the permit comes from Pima County instead of the Town, so ask your plumber which jurisdiction applies to your property.
