Emergency Sewer Line Cleaning in Marana, AZ

Emergency sewer line cleaning in Marana, AZ clears the main drain that carries all of your home's wastewater out to the public sewer. When it blocks, every fixture in the house is affected, and our 24/7 emergency line takes the call.

A main line clog is different from a sink clog. It sits past the branch lines, so it needs a machine that can reach it from a cleanout.

When the main sewer line is backing up into the house

Sewage rising in the lowest drains, a gurgling toilet and several fixtures failing at once all point to the main. Call (855) 575-2890. We answer 24/7, and a main line backup rarely improves on its own.

  1. Stop all water use and do not flush.
  2. Keep family and pets away from affected floors.
  3. Do not use chemical openers in a full line.
  4. Open a window to ventilate if the smell is strong.
  5. Move belongings off the floor in the lowest bathroom or laundry area.

Sewer backup: what to do first and who to call

Start by checking the pattern. If only the tub in the lowest bathroom is backing up, the main is the likely culprit. If a single sink is slow, you probably have a branch clog instead.

Next, look outside. A cleanout cap in the yard that shows standing water or a cap that is wet suggests the blockage sits between the house and the street.

Ask neighbors whether they have backups too. If several homes are affected, the public main may be the cause, and the utility is the right call. Marana Water Reclamation, or Pima County RWRD at (520) 724-6609 in areas such as Continental Reserve, Hartman Vistas and Cortaro, handle the mains.

If it is only your house, the private lateral is yours to maintain, and we can clear it.

Main line cleaning: how we clear it

We work from the cleanout whenever possible, since it gives a straight path to the main. A large drum machine pushes a heavy cable with cutting heads through the blockage, and then we use a jetting hose or a retrieval tool to clear what is left.

For repeat blockages or heavy grease, hydro jetting scours the walls, which a cutting head does not. If the cable hits a hard stop each time, we pause and look with a camera before forcing anything. That decision protects the pipe and your yard.

Monsoon runoff and your sewer line

Monsoon season officially runs June 15 to September 30. Heavy rain can wash silt, sand and leaves into open cleanouts and yard drains. Stormwater should never enter the sanitary sewer, but loose cleanout caps and damaged lines let it in.

Check that your cleanout cap is tight and the area around it drains away from the pipe before the season starts. After a big storm, look for standing water around the cap and clear the debris.

Storm drain cleaning

Storm drains carry rainwater and are separate from the sanitary sewer. Private yard, patio and driveway drains can clog with sand, leaves and trash, and we can clear them. Storm drains in public streets belong to the public agency, so ask the Town of Marana Development Services at (520) 382-2600 who handles yours.

Grease traps and restaurant lines

Commercial kitchens send fats, oils and grease into the line every day. A grease interceptor catches much of it, but it must be emptied on a schedule, usually by a licensed hauler. We clear the lines to and from the trap and can set up recurring cleaning for restaurants, which keeps a minor buildup from becoming a closure.

Main sewer cleaning for older lines and roots

Roots enter at joints and crack lines, and they grow back after clearing. A line that needs main sewer cleaning more than once or twice a year is telling you something. That is the point to inspect it and consider sewer line repair. Customers outside the Town, such as Picture Rocks, should confirm whether they are on public sewer or septic first.

Cleanouts and slope are part of what the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) asks for, and the Town of Marana has adopted it, so a line without an accessible cleanout may need one added before cleaning is done properly.

After the line is cleared

A few habits keep it open longer.

  • Keep wipes, paper towels and feminine products out of the toilet, even if labeled flushable.
  • Collect cooking grease in a can and trash it, not the sink.
  • Keep the cleanout accessible and the cap on tight.
  • Watch trees and shrubs planted near the lateral.
  • Note any slow drains early and call before a full backup.
  • Ask about a camera pass if clogs return within a few months.
