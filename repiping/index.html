Repiping in Marana, AZ

Repiping in Marana, AZ means replacing the water supply pipes in a home, either all of them or the failing section, with new pipe. It is a planned project, not a first response, and for many homes it is not needed at all.

Most Marana homes are newer, so repiping here is mostly about a specific pipe material that is failing or scale that has choked a line. If you have had repeat leaks or rust-colored water, call (855) 575-2890 and we will work out whether replacement actually makes sense.

Do you really need a repipe?

Often, the honest answer is no. A single leak at a fitting or a failed valve is a repair, not a reason to replace every pipe in the house. We would rather fix what is broken and leave sound pipe alone.

Repiping becomes worth discussing when the pipe itself is the problem, because patching one spot only moves the failure down the line.

Signs the pipes themselves are wearing out

One of these alone may be explained. Several together point toward the pipe.

Pinhole leaks that keep coming back

A repair holds, and then another small leak appears a few feet away. That pattern suggests the pipe wall is thinning.

Rust-colored or brown water

Discolored water, especially at first use in the morning, can come from corroding steel pipe.

Weak flow at several fixtures

Buildup inside the pipe narrows the opening and lowers flow, even when the street pressure is fine.

Green or blue stains on fittings

Pitting and staining around copper joints can mean corrosion from the inside.

Galvanized pipe and the pipes behind the walls

Galvanized steel pipe has a zinc coating that wears away, and the steel then rusts from the inside. The pipe narrows, flow drops, and eventually the wall gives out. It is typically found in older construction, and many newer Marana homes use different materials, so we confirm what you have before recommending anything.

We do not guess a pipe material from the neighborhood. We look where the pipe enters the house and wherever it is exposed in the attic or garage.

What Marana water and construction add to the picture

The Town's May 2026 table shows hardness from roughly 3 to 14 grains per gallon depending on the neighborhood. Harder water leaves more scale, which narrows pipe and wears valves. Your water bill or the Town lookup shows which system serves you.

Most newer Marana homes are built on concrete slabs, so supply lines often run in or under the slab or through the attic. When lines run under a slab, a repipe may reroute them overhead, the same idea behind slab leak repair by rerouting.

Whole-house repipe or a partial replacement

The scope should follow the condition of the pipe, not a sales target.

Partial repipe

  • Replaces one failing run, such as the line to a bathroom or the section in the attic.
  • Less access work, shorter water shutoff, and less repair of walls and paint.
  • Leaves older pipe in place, so other sections can still fail later.

Whole-house repipe

  • Replaces all supply lines from the main to every fixture.
  • Ends the repeat-repair cycle on the old system.
  • Needs more access openings in drywall and attic, and a longer period with the water off.

PEX or copper: the honest trade-offs

Both are accepted materials under the plumbing code Marana adopted. The right one depends on the house, the water, and your preferences.

Comparing the two most common replacement pipes
QuestionPEXCopper
How does it install?Flexible tubing that bends around corners, with fewer jointsRigid pipe joined with fittings, which takes more labor in tight spaces
How does it handle hard water?Does not corrode from the inside, though scale can still coat fixturesCan resist scale but may pit in some water, so conditions matter
How does it handle heat?Must be protected from sunlight and follow manufacturer limits in hot atticsHandles heat well and is not affected by sunlight
How does it hold up to freezes?Tolerates some expansion, but exposed runs still need protectionMore likely to split if water freezes inside

We explain which fits your house and why. There is no universal winner.

How a repipe project runs

Every house is different, but the sequence is usually the same.

  1. 1

    Inspect and confirm

    We check the existing pipe, test pressure, and look for leaks. If a hidden leak is suspected, we use leak detection first so we do not replace pipe that was fine.

  2. 2

    Pull the permit

    Marana lists plumbing permit and inspection through Development Services at (520) 382-2600. For Picture Rocks and other unincorporated areas, Pima County handles it, and our Picture Rocks page has the local detail.

  3. 3

    Replace the lines

    New pipe goes in, connections are made at fixtures and the water heater, and old pipe is capped or removed.

  4. 4

    Test and close up

    We pressure-test, restore water, and patch the access openings. Paint and texture matching are separate trades.

What to do before you call about repiping

  • List the symptoms you see, such as discolored water, low pressure, or pinhole leaks, and where they happen.
  • Take photos of any visible pipe, fittings and corrosion for the plumber.
  • Ask whether your homeowner's insurance policy has any say in the repipe work.
  • Plan for the water to be off for part of a day and store drinking water in advance.

Drywall, attic access, and how long the water is off

A whole-house repipe usually means a number of small openings in drywall and time in the attic. A partial job may need only one or two access points.

How long the water is off depends on the size of the house and the number of fixtures. We give you a plan for each stage, but we cannot promise a fixed duration before seeing the home. Repiping is permitted work under the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by the Town of Marana, so a written quote should say who pulls the permit and when inspections happen.
