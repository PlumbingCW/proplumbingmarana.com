A toilet that will not stop overflowing is an emergency

If water is rising in the bowl or spreading across the floor, stop it first and call second. Our 24/7 line is staffed for exactly this, and you can call from the bathroom while the water is off.

  1. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or close the shutoff valve behind the toilet.
  2. Turn the oval or round handle clockwise until it stops. Do not force it if it is stuck.
  3. If the valve will not close, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  4. Keep family members and pets out of the room if the water might be sewage.
  5. Call (855) 575-2890 and tell us whether the water is clean, dirty or coming from the wall.

How to find and close a toilet shutoff valve

Every toilet should have its own shutoff valve, usually on the wall or floor just behind and to the left of the bowl. Knowing where it is turns a flood into a small mess.

  1. 1

    Look low on the wall

    The valve is a small oval or round handle on a thin supply line running from the wall or floor to the bottom of the tank.

  2. 2

    Turn clockwise

    Right is tight. Turn slowly until the handle stops. Older valves can seize, so stop if you feel it grinding.

  3. 3

    Flush to check

    Flush once. If the tank empties and does not refill, the valve works and the tank is isolated.

What the toilet is telling you

These are the symptoms we hear about most. Some can wait for a daytime visit, and some cannot.

Water around the base

A damp ring or puddle at the floor often points to a failed wax ring or a loose closet bolt. The floor under it may be wet.

Running or hissing

A tank that refills every few minutes has a leaking flapper or a worn fill valve. It wastes water all day and shows up on the bill.

Clogs that keep coming back

One clog is a bad flush. A toilet that clogs every few days may have a partial blockage downstream or a weak flush.

Water in the wall or ceiling

Stains below a bathroom or a damp wall behind the tank mean a supply line, valve or drain joint is leaking out of sight. Shut the water off now.

Wax rings, loose bolts and leaks at the floor

A toilet sits on a wax ring or foam seal that closes the gap between the bowl outlet and the drain flange. Rock the toilet and it can crack that seal. A leak at the base can also come from a cracked bowl, a loose tank or a bad supply connection that drips down the outside.

Most newer Marana homes are built on concrete slabs, so a bathroom floor often has tile over slab and little warning when it gets wet. Water can spread under the tile edge or into the baseboard before you see a stain. If the floor feels soft, smells musty or the grout is darkening, tell us on the phone.

Hard water, scale and the fill valve

Marana Water runs several separate systems, so hardness depends on your neighborhood. Town figures put it at roughly 3 to 14 grains per gallon depending on where you live. Gladden Farms, on the Marana Municipal system, averages about 9 grains per gallon, and the Picture Rocks system averages about 12.

The Town says this hardness poses no health risk, but it leaves deposits. In a toilet tank those deposits collect on the fill valve, the flapper seat and the small ports that let water in. A valve that sticks open or closed can make the tank run constantly, refill slowly or trickle all night.

Cleaning a scaled valve helps for a few months at best. Fill valves and flappers are inexpensive parts, so we usually replace them when scale is heavy.

Clogs, overflows and when it is the drain

A plunger and a toilet auger fix most single clogs. If the bowl rises again within minutes, or other drains are slow, the blockage is probably in the branch line or main line. Our drain cleaning work uses a cable machine to reach what a plunger cannot.

If sewage comes up in a tub or floor drain, the blockage is beyond the toilet. Stop using water in the house and call.

A running toilet is a leak you cannot see

A flapper that leaks slowly can lose a lot of water over a month. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait. Color in the bowl without a flush means the flapper leaks. For other causes of a high bill, our leak detection service tests the whole system.
