When a failed tank is leaking right now

A tank that is leaking from the shell, or that has water pouring from its base, cannot be patched. Call the 24/7 emergency line, then take the steps below to protect your floors.

  1. Shut the cold water valve above the heater. If it will not turn, close the main water shutoff for the house.
  2. Switch off the breaker that feeds an electric heater. Do not touch the heater or the wiring if you are standing in water.
  3. On a gas heater, turn the gas control to off only if the area is dry and there is no gas odor. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 and Southwest Gas at 877-860-6020 first.
  4. Open a hot tap upstairs to let pressure out, and use a hose or a shop vacuum to move water toward a drain or outdoors.
  5. Take photos of the tank, the label and the damage. They help with insurance and with ordering the right replacement.

What a replacement day looks like

Before any work, we confirm fuel type, tank size, and the space the new unit has to fit. The old tank is drained, disconnected, and carried out. Then the new one is set on a drain pan with a discharge line, connected to the water lines, and tied into gas or power.

From shutoff to first hot shower

Replacement follows a set order. Each step protects something about the next one.

  1. 1

    Isolate and drain

    We close the water and fuel, then drain the old tank to a floor drain or outdoors. A flooded unit may need pumping first.

  2. 2

    Remove and inspect connections

    Old flex connectors, shutoff valves and any corroded nipples come off. Worn parts are better replaced while everything is open.

  3. 3

    Set the new tank

    The pan goes down first, and the tank is leveled on it. The relief valve discharge line runs to a safe place.

  4. 4

    Connect water, fuel and venting

    We tie in supply lines, then gas with a leak test or electric with the right breaker. A gas heater also needs a vent that draws properly.

  5. 5

    Fill, purge and test

    The tank fills with a hot tap open to push out air. Then we light or energize it, check for leaks, and confirm hot water at the fixtures.

  6. 6

    Inspection

    Where a permit is required, the safety inspection follows. We tell you what the inspector will look for.

Your replacement options

Three paths cover most Marana homes. Each has real trade-offs, and we do not push one.

Standard tank, gas or electric

The simplest swap, usually the lowest first cost, and the closest to what you have now. It stores hot water, so it loses a little heat even when idle.

Tankless

It heats water as you use it and takes less space. Hard water scale is the main upkeep in Marana, and gas or electrical capacity must support it. See our tankless water heater page.

Heat pump (hybrid)

It moves heat from the surrounding air into the tank, which can use less electricity. It needs open air space and a condensate drain, and it runs best in a room that does not get blocked in.

Garage heat and tank location

Most newer Marana homes are built on concrete slabs, so the heater often sits in a garage or attic. Tucson normals put June highs near 101 degrees, and those spaces run hotter. A heat pump unit pulling air from a hot garage is a sensible fit, while a tank crammed into a tight attic is harder to service and more likely to damage a ceiling if it fails.

Why the old tank wore out

Hard water speeds this up. The Town's May 2026 table puts the range at roughly 3 to 14 grains per gallon, depending on the system, with Gladden Farms around 9 and Picture Rocks around 12. Minerals settle on the tank floor and the anode rod gives up its metal to protect the steel.

A new tank in a hard-water neighborhood does better with a periodic flush, an anode check, and an expansion tank if the house has a check valve or pressure reducing valve. Our water softener and filtration page covers the other end of the problem. The Town of Marana has adopted the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) with local amendments.

A replacement unit should be set with the relief valve and discharge line that code expects, and your plumber should confirm whether a permit applies.
