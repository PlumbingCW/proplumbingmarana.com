Water Heater Pressure Relief Valve: What Marana Homeowners Should Know

The temperature and pressure relief valve, or T&P valve, is the safety device on a water heater that opens when the tank gets too hot or the pressure climbs too high. It sits near the top of the tank, with a metal discharge pipe running down from it. Its job is to vent water before the tank can fail.

Never cap, plug or block that valve or its pipe. If it drips, leaks steadily or sticks, treat that as a sign the heater needs attention. This guide covers how it works, how to test it, and when to call a plumber in Marana, AZ.

Scalding and safety first

Water from a relief valve can be hot enough to cause serious burns in a second or two. Keep your hands, face and feet clear of the discharge pipe opening, and keep children and pets away during any test.

If the tank is rumbling, the pipes are banging or steam is escaping, do not touch the valve. Turn the water heater off at its control or breaker, close the cold supply valve if you can reach it safely, and call a plumber.

What the valve does in plain terms

Inside the valve is a spring that holds a seat closed against tank pressure. If pressure or temperature rises past the valve's rating, the spring gives way and water flows out through the discharge pipe. That keeps the tank from becoming a pressure vessel with nowhere to go.

It is a last line of defense. Normally the thermostat shuts the heater off long before the valve opens. If the valve opens often, something upstream is failing or the plumbing system is trapping pressure.

Summer heat, monsoon season and permits in Marana

Tucson's average highs run about 101 degrees in June and 100 in July, according to National Weather Service 1991 to 2020 normals. A garage or attic can be far hotter than that. Plan to inspect the heater, valve and discharge pipe before summer, when heat puts the most stress on them.

Monsoon season officially runs June 15 to September 30. Storm surges and power flickers can trip controls, so if your heater acts oddly after a storm, check the thermostat and the relief valve. A valve that opens after a storm is a reason to have the system checked, not ignored.

Marana lists water heater replacement under its permit application process, with a safety inspection afterward. Ask Development Services at (520) 382-2600 how it applies to your project. In unincorporated areas such as Picture Rocks, Pima County rules apply, and our Picture Rocks plumber page explains service there.

Rules of thumb for the discharge pipe

The pipe matters as much as the valve. These are general practices, and your installer and local code decide the details.

  • The pipe runs downward the entire way, with no upward bends or traps.
  • It is the same size as the valve outlet or larger, never smaller.
  • It ends in an open, visible spot, usually a few inches above a floor drain or outside the building.
  • It has no valve, cap, plug or threaded end on it.
  • It is made from a material approved for hot water.
  • It does not discharge onto a walking surface where someone could be burned.

How to test the lever safely

Many manufacturers suggest an occasional test. Check your tank's label and manual first, and skip the test if the valve looks corroded or the tank is old.

  1. 1

    Confirm the pipe is in place

    Make sure the discharge pipe runs to a safe spot and that a bucket sits under the open end.

  2. 2

    Stand clear

    Keep your body to the side of the pipe opening, not in front of it.

  3. 3

    Lift the lever briefly

    Raise the lever for a second or two, then let it snap back. A short burst of water should flow into the bucket.

  4. 4

    Watch it reseat

    The flow should stop when you release the lever. A dribble that continues means the seat may be fouled.

  5. 5

    Check for leaks later

    Look again an hour later. A drip after the test points to scale on the seat, and the valve may need replacement.

Thermal expansion explained

Water expands when it heats. In a closed system, such as one with a check valve or a pressure reducing valve at the house inlet, that extra volume has nowhere to go. Pressure rises and the relief valve may drip each time the heater runs.

An expansion tank absorbs that extra volume. If your valve drips on a regular cycle, ask a plumber whether the system has one, whether it is charged properly, and whether the supply pressure is within a normal range.

Hot garages, hot attics and heater controls

Most newer Marana homes are built on concrete slabs, so water heaters often sit in garages or attics. Both spaces get very hot in summer. High ambient heat is hard on valves, thermostats and plastic fittings, and it can make a tank run warmer than expected.

Keep the area around the heater clear for airflow. Do not store paper, paint or fuel next to it. If your thermostat setting seems to climb on its own, a failing control may be the cause and the valve may be the only thing preventing a bigger problem.

Replacing a relief valve: when it is a DIY job and when it is not

Replacing the valve is a small task in theory, but it involves a pressurized tank and hot water.

Always shut off the power or gas, close the cold supply and relieve pressure before removing a valve. Use a replacement with the same pressure and temperature rating as the original.

When to call a plumber

Call if the valve leaks constantly, will not close, will not move, or if the discharge pipe is missing, capped or aimed at a floor. Our water heater repair page explains what a diagnosis covers.

If the tank is old and corroded, a new valve may only delay the bigger repair. Our water heater installation page explains what a proper new setup includes, relief valve and discharge pipe included. The 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) that the Town of Marana adopted calls for a relief valve with a properly routed discharge pipe on a water heater, so never cap it and replace it with a matching rated valve.
