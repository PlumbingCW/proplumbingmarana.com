How to judge any plumber before you hire

Use this list with us or with anyone else. A good plumber will not mind the questions.

  • Look up the license on the Arizona Registrar of Contractors site at roc.az.gov and read the record yourself.
  • Confirm the license class covers the work. Plumbing classes are C-37, CR-37 and R-37.
  • Ask for the price in writing or by message before work starts, and ask what could change it.
  • Ask who pulls the permit when one is required, and what inspection follows.
  • Be cautious if a plumber promises an exact arrival time over the phone for a problem they have not seen.

The ROC says to make certain the contractor's license class allows for the work listed in the contract. That one check catches many problems early.

Licensing classes in plain terms

Arizona divides plumbing licenses by who the work is for. CR-37 is a dual license for residential and commercial plumbing, C-37 covers commercial plumbing, and R-37 is for residential plumbing including solar. R-37 has subclassifications such as gas piping, sewers and drains, and swimming pool plumbing.

The C-37 scope also includes septic tanks and leaching lines, water conditioning equipment, lawn sprinklers and hot water supply systems. Because the scope varies, a license that fits a water heater job may not fit every other job.

Permits are part of doing the job right

Inside Marana, the Town adopted the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) with local amendments. Marana lists water heater replacement under its permit application process with a safety inspection, so confirm details with Development Services at (520) 382-2600.

Outside the Town, Pima County lists permits for new or replacement water heaters and for new pipe work. A plumber who skips a required permit can leave you with a problem when you sell or file an insurance claim.

Our honest limits

We fix plumbing. We do not diagnose health effects of water, and we do not replace advice from your water provider, your insurer or a building inspector. Some jobs, like a buried line under a driveway, need more than a quick visit.

If a problem is outside what we do, we will say so on the phone rather than send someone out.
