Water Heater Installation in Marana, AZ

Water heater installation in Marana, AZ is the planned job of choosing where a heater goes, how it is vented and powered, and how it meets local code. We handle new homes, remodels and upgrades, and we are licensed and insured.

A good install is mostly decided before the tank arrives. The location, the fuel, and the small safety parts around the heater matter as much as the heater.

Where the heater goes decides most of the job

Most newer Marana homes sit on concrete slabs, so heaters usually live in a garage, a utility closet or an attic. Tucson June highs average about 101 degrees, and garages and attics run hotter than the air outside. Heat shortens the life of seals and electronic controls.

A garage floor location is the easiest to service and the easiest to drain if it leaks. An attic location needs a sturdy platform, a drain pan with a discharge path, and room to work. We look at the space with you before any equipment is ordered.

What we check before we set a tank

This list is what separates a tidy install from a callback.

  • Fuel type: natural gas service at the address, or electric only
  • Gas line size and shutoff, if the heater is gas-fired
  • Electrical capacity and a dedicated breaker, if the heater is electric or heat pump
  • Venting path and clearances for a gas unit, and airflow room for a heat pump unit
  • Water pressure at the home, and whether a pressure reducing valve is present
  • Space for a drain pan and a relief valve discharge line to a safe place
  • Whether an existing check valve means an expansion tank is needed

Thermal expansion and pressure

Water expands as it heats. If the home has a pressure reducing valve or a check valve at the meter, that expanded water has nowhere to go, and pressure spikes inside the tank. An expansion tank gives it a place to go, and a pressure reducing valve keeps incoming pressure in a safe range.

A dripping relief valve is often a pressure problem more than a valve problem. We measure pressure at install time so the new heater is not stressed from its first day.

Drain pan, discharge line and the small parts that save floors

A relief valve is a safety device, and its discharge line has to run somewhere safe. A drain pan under a heater, piped to a proper drain or outdoors where practical, gives a slow leak somewhere to go besides your garage wall.

Flex connectors, shutoff valves and dielectric fittings go in new. Reusing 15-year-old supply connectors on a new tank is a leak waiting to happen.

Code adoption, permits and inspection

Town Code adopts the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) with local amendments, along with the International Fuel Gas Code and a 2023 NEC edition for electrical work. The local amendments are posted on the Town's building safety page, and we install to what is adopted.

Marana lists water heater replacement under its permit application process with a safety inspection. For details, contact Development Services at (520) 382-2600. Unincorporated areas such as Picture Rocks follow Pima County, which lists a new or replacement water heater as requiring a permit. Our Picture Rocks page covers that area.

New construction and master-planned communities

Communities such as Gladden Farms and Saguaro Bloom are on Town water systems with different hardness figures, and a builder's home may already have a heater set in place. We make no claims about any builder's equipment. If you are replacing or adding a heater in a newer home, tell us the make, size and fuel on the label.

Hard water and what to plan around it

Hardness varies across Marana. Marana Municipal at Gladden Farms averages about 9 grains per gallon, Picture Rocks and Saguaro Bloom near 12, and Hartman Vistas around 5. High hardness leaves mineral scale in a tank, so we include a drain valve you can reach and discuss an anode rod check. A softener is another option, and our softener and filtration page explains it.
