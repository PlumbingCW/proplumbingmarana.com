Water or sewage in a business after hours

A leak in an empty building runs until someone finds it, and a drain backup can ruin stock and floors overnight. Our 24/7 line is open for exactly this, and the steps below help while you wait.

  1. Shut off the main water valve if a supply line is leaking. Know where it is before you need it.
  2. Stop using restrooms, sinks and floor drains if sewage is backing up.
  3. Move inventory, files and electronics off the floor, and stay clear of wet outlets.
  4. Take photos and video of the damage and the source before cleanup starts.
  5. Call (855) 575-2890 and tell us the building type, the symptom and what is shut off.

Commercial leak detection

Leaks on a commercial property are hard to spot. Water runs in walls, under slabs, in ceilings above tenants or in buried lines to the street. The first clue is often a meter that keeps turning after the building is closed, or a bill that jumps.

We find leaks with listening equipment, pressure tests and, where it helps, a camera. Be realistic about the result. Detection tells you where the water is going, but fixing it may need walls opened, a slab cut or a landlord's approval. Our leak detection page covers the methods in more detail.

Practical tip: close the building, turn off irrigation and note the meter reading twice, an hour apart. A moving dial with everything off means water is leaking somewhere.

Commercial drain service and storm drains

A restaurant or retail drain carries more load than a home drain, and the line may serve several tenants. We clear stoppages with cable machines and, for heavy buildup, hydro jetting. A camera inspection shows whether the blockage is a one-time event or a damaged pipe.

Storm drains near parking lots catch debris, silt and trash, especially during monsoon season, which runs June 15 to September 30. We can clean a clogged storm drain on a property, though public storm systems belong to the Town or the county, not to us.

Grease trap cleaning

A grease trap or interceptor catches fats, oil and grease before they reach the sewer. When it fills, the kitchen drains slowly and then backs up, often during a rush. Cleaning means pumping out the trap and checking the baffles and lines.

Frequency depends on how much the kitchen cooks and what your local rules require, so ask your provider or the Town about your schedule. We do not set that schedule for you. Keep a log of each cleaning, because inspectors may ask for it.

Backflow testing for businesses

Many commercial properties have backflow preventers on the irrigation line, fire line or main service. These valve assemblies keep dirty water from flowing back into the supply, and providers often require testing each year. Confirm the rule with your water provider, because it differs by property.

Marana Water runs several separate systems, so the provider for a Tangerine Road shop may differ from a business near the airport. Check your bill or the Town's service lookup before assuming.

Plumbing across Marana's business corridors

Retail on Tangerine Road and Thornydale Road, the Gladden Farms shopping center and the Interstate 10 corridor all have different plumbing needs, from restaurant kitchens to roadside restrooms. Marana Regional Airport sits on its own Marana Water system with the hardest water in the Town table, 13 to 15 grains per gallon, so scale builds quickly on heaters, valves and fixtures there.

Hard water means more descaling and shorter life for water heaters and flush valves. Businesses on the airport system should plan for that in maintenance.

For property managers and landlords

When a tenant calls you, a few steps keep the repair, the insurance claim and the tenant relationship clean.

  • Confirm who owns the pipe. Tenants usually handle fixtures, while owners handle building lines, but leases vary.
  • Ask the tenant to photograph the damage and note when it started.
  • Shut off the nearest valve or the main, and tell other tenants if water is off.
  • Call (855) 575-2890 with the address and a contact who can authorize work.
  • Keep our written scope and invoice for your insurer and the lease file.
