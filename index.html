The first minutes of a plumbing emergency

A burst pipe or a failed water heater can move a lot of water in a short time. The steps below limit the damage while you call our 24/7 emergency line.

If you smell gas, leave the building first and call 911 and the gas utility before you call us. Stay away from outlets and panels if water is near them.

  1. Shut off the main water valve. Its location varies from house to house, so find yours before you ever need it.
  2. Turn off the water heater power or gas control if the tank is leaking, and keep clear of wet electrical equipment.
  3. Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet area, and open a drain or hose bib to relieve pressure if you can.
  4. Take photos of the damage before you clean up, then call (855) 575-2890 and describe what you see.

What Marana water means for pipes and heaters

Marana water comes from groundwater in the Lower Santa Cruz part of the Tucson Basin Aquifer. Several separate Marana Water systems serve the Town, and their hardness differs. The Town's May 2026 table runs from roughly 3 to 14 grains per gallon depending on the neighborhood.

That range matters because hard water leaves mineral scale on heating elements, tank bottoms, faucet aerators and shower heads. A home in the Gladden Farms area, at about 9 grains per gallon on average, sees less scale than the Marana airport system at 13 to 15. The Town says hardness poses no health risk, and we do not judge health effects of water.

If you shop for a softener, check whether the product uses ppm or grains per gallon. One grain per gallon is about 17.1 ppm. Your provider may differ by address, so confirm it on your water bill or with the Town's lookup before you rely on any figure.

Newer Marana homes are mostly built on concrete slabs, so supply and drain lines often run in or under the slab or through the attic. That makes a hidden leak harder to spot, and it is why a climbing water bill deserves attention early.

Neighborhoods we hear from

Gladden Farms is a master-planned community whose first homes date to 2004, and it now has more than 3,000 homes. Its Marana Municipal water averages about 9 grains per gallon. Water heaters in homes of that age may already have been replaced once, so look at the label on yours.

Continental Ranch was annexed by Marana in 1987 and has about 4,000 HOA homes near the Santa Cruz River and Cortaro Road. Building there began after 1992, which means many homes now have decades-old fixtures and shutoff valves. The Continental Reserve area sits on the Picture Rocks water system at about 12 grains per gallon.

Dove Mountain opened in 1998 in the Tortolita foothills and has about 5,000 homes. We do not have a published hardness figure for it, so check your bill for your provider. Saguaro Bloom is a new D.R. Horton community in northwest Marana, still under development in 2026, and it sits on the Airline/Lambert system at about 12 grains per gallon.

Picture Rocks lies in unincorporated Pima County, so its permits come from the county and not the Town. We also take calls from Rillito, Cortaro, Twin Peaks, Hartman Vistas, Oasis Hills, San Lucas, Linda Vista and Tortolita. The service areas page lays out ZIP codes and water systems.

Permits and checking any plumber's license

The Town of Marana adopts the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) with local amendments. Marana lists water heater replacement under its permit application process with a safety inspection, so confirm details with Development Services at (520) 382-2600. In Picture Rocks, Pima County rules apply instead.

Before you hire anyone, look up the license at roc.az.gov, the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. Plumbing classes include CR-37 for dual residential and commercial work, C-37 for commercial plumbing and R-37 for residential plumbing. The ROC advises you to make certain the license class allows the work in your contract.

Arizona 811 asks you to call 811 at least two working days before any digging, which matters if a buried line needs work. Ask any plumber who handles that call and when.
