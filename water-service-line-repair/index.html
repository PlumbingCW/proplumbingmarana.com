Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Marana, AZ

Your water service line is the buried pipe that carries water from the meter to your house, and when it fails the signs are a soft wet patch in the yard, weak pressure at every tap, or a meter that keeps running. Emergency water service line repair in Marana, AZ means finding the break, shutting the line down safely, and repairing or replacing the pipe. Call (855) 575-2890, 24/7.

Because the pipe is underground, the repair involves digging or a trenchless method, and it involves a few questions about who owns which part of the line. This page covers both.

When the line from the meter is leaking or has broken

A buried line that has split can wash out soil, flood a yard, or drop pressure to a trickle. Our 24/7 line is open at (855) 575-2890, and these steps limit the loss while you wait.

  1. Close the main shutoff at the house. If water keeps running, the loss is upstream of that valve.
  2. If the leak is on the meter side of your valve, call Marana Water at (520) 382-2570 during business hours, or (520) 235-4381 after hours, weekends, and holidays for leaks.
  3. Do not dig to find the leak. Buried utilities may be nearby.
  4. Keep children and pets away from soft, sunken, or flooded ground.
  5. Photograph the wet area and the meter dial.

How a failing service line shows itself

A buried line seldom announces itself loudly. These are the usual clues.

A wet or sunken patch

Water finds the easiest path up. In hard desert soil it may surface well away from the break.

Meter moves with the house shut off

If the indicator spins after you close the main valve, the leak is between the meter and that valve.

Pressure drops everywhere

A major leak can starve the whole house, including showers and outdoor spigots.

A higher bill with no new use

A steady leak adds up month after month, and it never shows indoors.

Dirty or sandy water

A break can pull soil into the line when pressure changes, so cloudy water can be a warning.

Who is responsible for which side of the meter

The Town says there are several water providers within its limits, so the answer depends on your address. Check your water bill, use the Town's service-area lookup, or ask Marana Water at (520) 382-2570 where the utility's responsibility ends and yours begins.

The meter is the usual reference point for a handoff, but practice varies by provider, so confirm with them before you assume. We can tell you which side of the meter the evidence points to.

Low water pressure that is not a leak

Weak pressure does not always mean a buried break. A partly closed main valve, a failed pressure regulator, and scale inside old pipes can all do it. Hard water leaves deposits over time, and the Town's table shows roughly 3 to 14 grains per gallon depending on the neighborhood, so scale is more of a factor in some areas than others.

A quick check helps. If pressure is low at every fixture, including outdoors, the cause is at or before the main. If only one fixture is weak, the problem is probably local to it. When the meter also moves with the house shut off, the line is the likely culprit.

Trench or trenchless: choosing the repair

There are two ways to fix a buried line. The best one depends on the soil, what sits above the pipe, and the condition of the line.

Comparing trench and trenchless repair for a residential water service line
ApproachHow it worksHonest trade-offs
Open trenchDig down along the pipe, remove the damaged section or the whole run, and lay new pipeEasy to inspect and connect, but it disturbs landscaping, walkways, and driveways, and hard desert soil slows the digging
Spot dig and repairDig a small pit at the located break and replace one sectionLess disruption, but it only fixes that spot, so it fits a line that is otherwise sound
Trenchless replacementPull a new pipe through the path of the old one from short access pits at each endSaves hardscape and landscaping, but needs a straight enough path and suitable conditions, so not every site qualifies

Where the line crosses a driveway or patio, trenchless can make a big difference. Where it runs through open ground, a simple trench may be quicker and cleaner.

Before any digging starts

Buried lines share the ground with gas, electric, and communication lines. Locating them is a legal step as well as a safety one.

  1. 1

    Call Arizona 811

    Call 811 or visit Arizona811.com at least two working days before digging. Arizona Corporation Commission wording says it is free, easy, and the law.

  2. 2

    Wait for the marks

    Utilities mark their lines with paint or flags. Do not dig until they have responded.

  3. 3

    Confirm the permit path

    Ask Town of Marana Development Services at (520) 382-2600 whether the work needs a permit. For unincorporated areas such as Picture Rocks, permits come from Pima County.

  4. 4

    Locate the break

    We pinpoint the leak with a meter test and listening gear before digging, which often keeps the excavation smaller. See leak detection for the methods.

  5. 5

    Repair, test, and restore

    We install the new pipe or repair, test it under pressure, and backfill. Landscaping and hardscape repair are discussed up front.

Hard desert soil and the work of digging

Desert ground can be dense and rocky, and hardpan layers slow a shovel and a trencher. That is one reason a located, targeted dig beats a guess. It also means a repair can take longer than the pipe length alone would suggest.

If the line keeps failing

One break on an old line is a repair. Repeated breaks mean the line is wearing out, and replacing the full run from meter to house may cost less over time. When the failures inside the house continue too, it is worth discussing burst pipe repair and a broader plan.

The line from the meter to the house is private plumbing governed by the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) in the Town of Marana, so a repair should be made with approved pipe and fittings and the permit question settled first.
