Water that will not shut off needs action now

A valve that will not close can fill a cabinet, soak a wall or flood a bathroom in minutes. Our 24/7 line is open at night and on weekends, and you can start the fix while you call.

  1. Close the small angle stop under the sink by turning it clockwise.
  2. If that stop is broken or spraying, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain pressure from the pipes.
  4. Move cabinet contents and towels, and keep power cords out of the wet area.
  5. Call (855) 575-2890 and tell us which fixture failed and whether the main is off.

Why hard water kills faucet cartridges in Marana

Town of Marana figures show hardness ranging from roughly 3 to 14 grains per gallon depending on the neighborhood. Gladden Farms averages about 9, while the Airport system runs 13 to 15. The Town says this poses no health risk, but the minerals leave scale on faucets and fixtures.

Scale hardens inside a single-handle cartridge and on the rubber seats of a ceramic disc. The handle gets stiff, the flow drops, and one day the valve will not seal. Aerators clog the same way, so a weak stream is often scale and not a pressure problem.

If your faucet fails after only a few years, hard water is the usual reason. A new cartridge costs less than a new faucet in most cases, but a faucet with heavy scale on the body may be worth replacing.

Faucet and valve problems we are called for

Each of these has a different cause, and a different fix.

Will not shut off

The handle moves but the water keeps running. The cartridge, ball or seat is worn or jammed with scale.

Drip from the spout

A steady drip wastes water and stains the basin. Worn seats and washers are the usual cause.

Leak under the sink

Water pooling in the cabinet may come from a supply line, a stop valve or the faucet base. Dry each part and watch which one wets first.

Handle leaks or wobbles

Water around the handle base means a worn cartridge or O-ring. Do not keep tightening.

Shower or tub valve stuck

A shower that stays warm after you turn it off, or a tub that drips from the spout, points to the valve behind the wall.

A broken or burst supply valve

The small stop valve under a sink or toilet is easy to ignore. Old multi-turn stops can seize, and a stop that has not moved in years may crack when you try to close it. If one sprays, the main is the only way to stop it.

Replacing a failed stop usually means closing the main, draining the line and swapping the valve and supply tube. Most newer Marana homes are on slabs, so lines run through attics or walls, and a stuck stop on a wall line may need an access cut. We tell you which before we start.

If the leak is from a pipe inside the wall and not the valve itself, see our burst pipe repair page. A wet wall or ceiling is a different problem from a leaking stop.

Shower valves, tub spouts and drains

A shower valve sits inside the wall, so a repair may mean opening a panel or tile. We check what access exists before quoting. A tub spout that drips while the shower runs can also be a diverter problem, not a valve failure.

Slow shower and tub drains are a related call. Hair and soap are the usual blockage, and our drain cleaning service clears the line when a store-bought remedy does not.

What to check before we arrive

A few details help us bring the right parts.

  • Note the faucet brand, if it is printed on the handle or base.
  • Take a photo of the faucet and the pipes under the sink.
  • Check whether the angle stop turns, and say if it is stuck.
  • Look for scale, white crust or green stains around the connections.
  • Say whether the water is on or off at the main.

When the faucet is not the real problem

Low pressure at every faucet in the house usually points past the fixture. A sudden drop with no change at the sink may be the supply line or a pressure issue upstream, and our water service line repair page explains that case.

In the Picture Rocks area, water runs about 12 grains per gallon on average, so cartridges and aerators scale quickly there too. Faucets and their supply stops are covered by the plumbing code the Town of Marana adopted, the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) with local amendments, which is one more reason to match parts to your fixture rather than forcing a fit.
