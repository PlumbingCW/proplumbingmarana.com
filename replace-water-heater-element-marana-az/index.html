How to Replace a Water Heater Element in Marana, AZ

To replace a water heater element in Marana, AZ, switch off the breaker and confirm with a tester that the power is dead. Let the tank cool, shut off the cold supply and drain the water below the element. Then fit a matching new element with a fresh gasket and refill the tank completely before restoring power.

You cannot swap an element without draining first. The element threads into the side of a full tank, so removing it opens a hole under pressure. This guide covers the steps, the tests and the points where you should stop and call a plumber.

The myth of changing an element without draining

Some videos make it look like a quick swap on a full tank. It is not. The element sits below the water line, so the level has to drop under it first. For the upper element you drain part of the tank, and for the lower you drain nearly all of it, or hot water and sediment pour onto you and the wiring.

Signs an element has failed

Check these before you buy parts. Most electric tanks have two elements, one high and one low.

No hot water at all

If the upper element or its thermostat fails, the whole tank can stay cold. Check the breaker and the reset button first.

Lukewarm water that runs out fast

A dead lower element often leaves you with only the top third of the tank hot, so showers run cold quickly.

Breaker trips when the heater runs

A shorted element can leak current to the tank and trip the breaker. Stop using the heater until it is checked.

Crackling or popping

Mineral scale on the element can make noise as water boils under the crust. It is often a sign of scale, not just a bad element.

Marana heat and water, and your element

NWS Tucson 1991 to 2020 normals put average highs near 101 F in June and about 100 F in July. Many Marana heaters sit in garages or attics, where summer air is hotter still. Give yourself time to let the tank cool and work early in the day.

Town of Marana Water lists several systems, and the hard ones read about 12 GPG, with the airport system at 13 to 15. Hard-water homes tend to build scale on elements faster, so pull the old element and check the crust to see how fast your tank scales.

Marana lists water heater replacement under its permit application process with a safety inspection. An element swap is a repair, but if you are unsure where the line sits, confirm with Development Services at (520) 382-2600.

Safety before you touch a wire

Water and 240 volt power are a dangerous pair. Do every item before the access panel comes off.

  • Turn off the water heater breaker, then confirm at the heater with a non-contact tester and a multimeter that the wires are dead. A breaker label can be wrong.
  • Wait for the tank to cool. Water inside can be hot enough to scald for hours.
  • Never power the element while it is out of water. A dry element burns out in seconds.
  • Wear gloves and eye protection, and keep a bucket and towels ready.
  • Work with a helper if the heater is in an attic. Summer attic heat and a ladder are a poor mix.
  • If you smell gas or the unit is gas-fired, stop. This guide is for electric tanks only.

The replacement, one step at a time

Match the new element to the old one by volts, watts and thread style. The data plate on the heater lists the rating.

  1. 1

    Cut power and shut the cold supply

    Switch off the breaker, verify dead, then close the cold inlet valve on top of the tank.

  2. 2

    Connect a hose and open a hot tap

    Attach a hose to the drain valve and run it to a floor drain or outdoors. Open a hot faucet in the house so air can enter and water can leave.

  3. 3

    Drain below the element

    Open the drain valve. For the upper element, drain until the tank is a bit below it. For the lower, drain the tank fully. Scale can clog the valve, so stop and call if it will not flow.

  4. 4

    Remove the panel, insulation and wires

    Take photos of the wiring first. Disconnect the wires and note which terminal each came from.

  5. 5

    Unscrew the element

    Use an element wrench or deep socket and turn counterclockwise. A seized element can break, which is one point to stop and call.

  6. 6

    Clean and fit the new one

    Wipe the tank opening, fit the new gasket, thread in the element by hand and snug it with the wrench.

  7. 7

    Refill completely

    Close the drain, open the cold inlet, and leave the hot tap running until water flows steadily without air spitting. Check for drips at the element.

  8. 8

    Restore power last

    Reconnect the wires, replace insulation and panel, then switch the breaker on. Give the tank time to heat.

Testing the element and thermostat

Test with power off and the wires removed from the element. Set a multimeter to ohms.

What multimeter readings usually mean
TestNormal readingIf it reads otherwise
Across the two element terminalsA few ohms up to about 15, depending on wattsNo reading or open means a failed element
One probe on a terminal, one on the tankNo reading (open)Any reading means a short to ground, replace it
Thermostat and high-limit reset buttonPower passes through once reset and cooledNo power through usually points to a thermostat or high-limit fault

Readings vary with element wattage, so compare with the label on the element or heater. If the numbers confuse you, that is a good time to hand it to a plumber.

Why elements fail in hard water

Minerals in the water bake onto the hot surface of the element and form a crust. The crust traps heat, and the element can overheat and burn out. A flush now and then and clean new parts help, but a softener addresses the cause.

A replacement element can last a long time if the tank is free of heavy scale and sediment. If the tank is old and crusty, the new element may fail again sooner. That is a good moment to compare repair against replacement.

When to call a plumber

Call if you cannot verify the power is off, the drain valve will not flow, the old element will not unscrew, or water leaks from the tank itself. A leak from the shell cannot be repaired, and the tank will need to be replaced.
