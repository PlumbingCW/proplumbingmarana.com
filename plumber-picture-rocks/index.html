Before we arrive: four things to do in Picture Rocks

With a longer drive, the first minutes are yours. These steps limit damage while an emergency plumber is on the way.

1

Shut off the water

Close the main valve for the house, or the valve at the fixture. If you are on a private well, switch off the pump at its breaker.

2

Kill power near water

Turn off breakers for any room with standing water. Stay out of flooded areas that reach outlets or appliances.

3

Open a tap to drain pressure

Open the lowest faucet to relieve the line, and shut off the water heater if it is leaking.

4

Photograph and describe

Take photos of the damage and note where the leak started. Have your address and nearest cross road ready.

Hard water on the Picture Rocks system

The Town of Marana's hardness table lists the Picture Rocks system, number 10-092, at 210 to 220 ppm, about 12 grains per gallon on average. That is harder than much of Marana, where the Town's figures run from roughly 3 to 14 grains per gallon depending on the neighborhood.

The Town says hardness poses no health risk, but it leaves deposits on fixtures, dishes and heating elements. In practice, water heaters in Picture Rocks collect scale faster, tank bottoms fill with sediment, faucet cartridges stiffen and aerators clog.

The system may not serve every property here. Check your water bill to confirm your provider. Some properties may use other sources, and we do not assume.

Permits here come from Pima County, not the Town

Picture Rocks is unincorporated, so building permits are handled by Pima County, not the Town of Marana. Pima County Building Safety is at 201 N Stone Avenue. Its permit guide lists a permit as required for a new or replacement water heater and for new pipe work.

The county also has a replacement permit program for same-size water heater swaps where the fuel supply does not change. Check the county's current rules before work begins, because they can change. If you are inside Town limits, call Marana Development Services at (520) 382-2600 instead.

Tell us your address when you call, and we will point you to the right office. Our plumbing installation page explains how permits fit into larger jobs.

Drain and sewer calls: confirm your system first

Not every property here is connected the same way. Confirm whether your property is on public sewer or a septic system, because it changes who maintains the line and what a backup means. We do not assume either.

If you are on septic, Pima County's onsite wastewater program regulates it. Either way, a slow drain that gurgles, or sewage coming up in the lowest tub or shower, is an urgent sign. Stop using water and call.

The community we drive to

Picture Rocks sits in the Avra Valley region along Picture Rocks Road, which crosses Saguaro National Park's Tucson Mountain District, and along Sandario Road. The 2020 Census counted 9,551 residents and 4,299 housing units, and QuickFacts shows about 94 percent of households own their homes.

That mostly means owners paying for their own repairs, so we give a clear price before work starts and explain options. We do not make assumptions about the age of homes here.

Summer heat and desert conditions

Tucson-area summers average highs near 100 degrees from June through August, and garages and attics get hotter than the air outside. Water heaters in those spaces work harder and wear faster. A tank near the end of its life tends to fail in summer.

Brief freezes happen too. About 12 days a year fall at or below 32 degrees in Tucson, so protect exposed outdoor pipes and hose bibs on those nights.
