Plumbing Installation in Marana, AZ

Plumbing installation in Marana, AZ means adding or replacing the pipes, fixtures and equipment that carry water, drain waste and, where a home has it, natural gas. It covers a new sink or bathroom, a line to an outbuilding, a water heater swap and rough-in work for new construction.

Good installation is mostly planning. Where the line runs, which code applies and who issues the permit all matter before the first fitting goes in.

What a plumbing installation job includes

Installation work falls into a few kinds. Fixture installs replace a sink, toilet, tub or shower valve in an existing space. Line installs add a new run of water supply or drain pipe, such as a bathroom addition, a second kitchen sink or an outdoor spigot.

Equipment installs include water heaters, softeners and backflow devices. We also do rough-in plumbing for new construction, where pipes go in before walls close.

We confirm what the job needs before we quote it. You get a clear price before work starts.

Smell gas? Leave first, then call

If you smell rotten eggs, even faintly, or hear a hiss or roar near a gas line, leave the building immediately. Tell everyone to leave and leave doors open. From a safe place, call 911 and Southwest Gas at 877-860-6020, day or night. Call us only after that.

Gas lines: safety first, installation second

Many Marana homes are all-electric, and gas service at a given address is not something we can assume. If your home has natural gas service, a new line for a range, dryer, fireplace or grill is a licensed-contractor job with a pressure test and inspection.

Do not strike matches, use light switches, thermostats or appliance controls, start an engine or use an automatic garage door if you suspect a leak. Any of these can make a spark. Southwest Gas takes leak calls from customers and non-customers alike.

We do not promise a response time for a gas leak, and a suspected leak is a call to the gas utility and 911 first. For planned work, such as a new gas stub or an inspection, call our line and we will confirm what the address has.

New construction and remodel plumbing

New construction plumbing starts with the slab. Drain and supply lines are placed before concrete is poured, then tested, and later trim-out sets the fixtures. In a remodel, we tie new lines into existing ones and plan around what is already in the walls or slab.

If a project involves repiping an older line or replacing a water heater at the same time, see our repiping and water heater installation work.

Fixtures that suit the water

Marana hardness runs from roughly 3 to 14 grains per gallon depending on the neighborhood. Faucets with exposed ceramic discs and fixtures with fine aerator screens clog sooner in the harder areas. Picking fixtures that are easy to clean and take replacement cartridges saves trouble later.

Water heaters often sit in garages or attics that get very hot in summer, so we plan clearances, drain pans and relief piping with that in mind.

Backflow prevention and testing

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that keeps dirty water from flowing back into the drinking supply. You may see one on a lawn sprinkler line, a commercial building or a service connection with a fire system. Many water providers require them to be tested every year.

Requirements differ by provider and by property type, so confirm with your water provider whether your device needs annual testing. We do not assert a Marana rule. If your device needs repair or replacement, we can install it. Exposed devices can freeze in the rare cold nights Tucson records.

Choosing an installer

Arizona licenses plumbing contractors by class. The Registrar of Contractors lists C-37 for commercial, CR-37 for dual residential and commercial, and R-37 for residential. Check the license record at roc.az.gov and confirm the class covers your work.

Ask for a written scope, who pulls the permit and how inspections are handled. We are licensed and insured, and we give a clear price before work starts.
