How Continental Ranch came to be part of Marana

In 1987, Marana's Town Council annexed 2,100 acres of Continental Ranch. The developer's companies went bankrupt in 1989, and Southwest Value Partners bought the land in 1992 and sold large parcels to homebuilders. Homeowners took over the HOA in 2002.

For plumbing, the useful part is the build timeline. Homes were built in phases after 1992 by different builders, so the oldest could be about three decades old. We do not assume pipe materials for any one street. We do assume that supply valves, fill valves and heaters in older homes are due a look.

Sewer questions: who owns which pipe

The Town's FAQ draws a line. Mains in Town rights of way and easements belong to the Town, and the private lateral from house to main belongs to the homeowner. That line decides who you call about a backup.

If a drain keeps clogging, drain cleaning clears the line you own. A sewer camera inspection shows whether the problem is in your lateral or points toward the main, and sewer line repair covers the lateral itself.

Older-home checks for Continental Ranch

  • Turn each fixture's shutoff valve. If one will not close fully, it is a failure waiting for a bad night.
  • Look for green or white crust at supply connections under sinks.
  • Check the heater label for its manufacture date.
  • Watch your water bill for an unexplained rise, which can signal a hidden leak.

HOA rules and permits

Continental Ranch has an HOA, and many HOAs review exterior changes. If work touches the outside of your home, read your HOA documents first. Permits are handled by the Town of Marana, and Development Services can be reached at (520) 382-2600.
