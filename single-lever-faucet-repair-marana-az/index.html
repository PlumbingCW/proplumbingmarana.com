Single-Lever Faucet Repair in Marana, AZ

A dripping or stiff single-lever faucet in Marana, AZ is usually fixed by replacing the cartridge inside it, and a weak stream is usually a scaled-up aerator. Close the water at the sink valves, remove the handle, pull the old cartridge, and bring it to a supply store to match the new one exactly.

Hard water speeds up the wear, so a faucet that has run for years in Marana often needs the cleaning as much as the part.

Which kind of single-lever faucet do you have

Most single-handle faucets use one of two internals. Knowing which saves a wasted trip to the store.

Cartridge type

  • A tube-shaped insert sits under the handle
  • Lever moves smoothly up, down and side to side
  • Drips from the spout usually mean a worn seal or scale on the cartridge
  • Replacement cartridges are specific to brand and model

Ball type

  • A round ball with slots sits under a cap and cam
  • Common on older kitchen faucets
  • Leaks at the base of the handle often point to worn seals and springs
  • Repair kits include seats, springs, and a new ball if needed

Two more types you may run into

Some single-handle faucets use a ceramic disc cartridge, where two polished discs slide against each other. They resist wear well, but scale or grit between the discs can make the lever stiff or leave a drip. Replacement is the same as for any cartridge, with the water off and the new part matched to the old.

Bathroom and shower valves sometimes use a pressure-balancing cartridge. The steps are similar, but the valve is in the wall, so go slowly and call if you cannot see a clean way to reach it.

Hard water and the faucets in Marana

The Town of Marana table puts the water at roughly 3 to 14 grains per gallon depending on the neighborhood. The Town says it leaves deposits on bath fixtures, dishes and glassware. In a 12 GPG area, that scale builds on cartridges and aerators faster, so check them often.

Homes on the Marana Municipal system near Gladden Farms average about 9 GPG, while the Hartman Vistas system averages about 5 GPG. A faucet that clogs every few months in one area may last longer in another, so adjust your cleaning interval to your system.

If scale is a constant fight, a softener on the main line protects every faucet, though it is a larger decision than a cartridge.

Replace the cartridge in order

Lay a towel in the sink so small parts do not fall down the drain.

  1. 1

    Shut off the water

    Close both angle stops under the sink, then open the faucet to relieve pressure. If a stop will not close, shut off the house main.

  2. 2

    Remove the handle

    Look for a small cap, button or set screw. Pry the cap off, then loosen the set screw with the matching hex or screwdriver and lift the handle away.

  3. 3

    Take off the cap and retaining nut

    Unscrew the decorative cap, then the nut or clip that holds the cartridge. Photograph each layer before moving on.

  4. 4

    Pull the cartridge

    Grip it and pull straight up. If it is stuck with scale, a gentle wiggle works better than force. Note which way it faces.

  5. 5

    Match the new part

    Take the old one to the store. Brand, size and the number of splines must all line up.

  6. 6

    Install and test

    Set the new cartridge in the same direction, replace the nut and handle, then open the stops slowly and check for drips.

Why single-lever faucets start to drip

Four causes cover most drips.

Worn rubber seals

Seals and O-rings harden and lose their grip over time, so water slips past when the lever is closed.

Mineral buildup

Scale roughens the cartridge and the seat, which prevents a tight seal even with new rubber.

Loose parts

A retaining nut that has backed off lets the cartridge shift and leak.

Cracked or pitted seats

A damaged seat will keep dripping until the cartridge or the valve body is replaced.

Clearing the aerator

The aerator is the small screen at the tip of the spout. Unscrew it by hand or with a rag-wrapped pair of pliers, then tap out the grit. Soak the pieces in white vinegar for an hour, scrub them with an old toothbrush and reassemble in the same order.

If the screen is torn or the white crust will not budge, buy a new one for a few minutes of work. Run the water for a bit before you put it back, so loose debris flushes out of the faucet.

Safety and small-parts cautions

Never force a seized fitting, since a cracked body turns a ten minute fix into a new faucet. Keep an eye on the supply lines and the angle stops while you work. If anything under the sink is spraying, shut the main and call.

Before you buy parts

A small checklist saves a second trip.

  • Note the brand, usually stamped on the handle or the body.
  • Take photos of the faucet from the front and under the handle.
  • Bring the old cartridge or ball kit to compare.
  • Buy a spare O-ring set and plumber's grease.
  • Check that the angle stops close fully before you start.
  • Clear the area under the sink so you can see and reach.

When to call a plumber

Call if the angle stop is seized or leaking, if the faucet body is corroded or if the new cartridge still drips. Also call if you find water inside the cabinet that you cannot trace, since a hidden leak can damage cabinet floors.
